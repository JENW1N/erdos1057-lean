LITERATURE AND CLAIM COMPARISON
The target is a count valid for every sufficiently large bound, equivalent to log C(x)/log x tending to 1. A finite list, infinitude alone, a fixed smaller power lower bound, or a conditional proof does not settle that target.

1. Alford, Granville, Pomerance (1994), There are infinitely many Carmichael numbers
Classical infinitude and power lower bounds; does not establish the full exponent-one target unconditionally.
https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/paper95.pdf

2. Lichtman (2022), Primes in arithmetic progressions to large moduli and shifted primes without large prime factors
Corollary 1.2 states at least x^0.3389 Carmichael numbers up to x. Also explains the Harman predecessor bound.
https://arxiv.org/html/2211.09641

3. Runbo Li, The number of primes in short intervals and numerical calculations for Harman’s sieve, v8 (2025)
Theorem 10 claims C(x)>x^0.34. Even accepting it, this is a partial lower bound, not exponent one; no independent verification of its sieve calculations in this review.
https://arxiv.org/html/2308.04458v8

4. Wright (2020), A conditional density for Carmichael numbers
Near-linear exponent under a strong least-prime-in-arithmetic-progressions assumption; conditional.
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/article/conditional-density-for-carmichael-numbers/E732DD46A7CF3D0EE22BB21D16D72645

5. Larsen (2021/2023), Bertrand’s Postulate for Carmichael Numbers
At least exp(log(x)/(log log(x))^(2+delta)) Carmichael numbers in certain short intervals; not the exponent-one global count.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06963

6. Larsen (2025), Carmichael Numbers in All Possible Arithmetic Progressions
Infinitude or emptiness criterion in all arithmetic progressions, and a totient-ratio consequence; not the full counting target.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09056

7. Larsen and Wright (2025), Carmichael Numbers with a Specified Number of Prime Factors
Existence with exactly R prime factors for every sufficiently large R; does not give the requested global count.
https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16632

8. Bharadwaj and Rodgers (2024 preprint / 2026 publication), Large prime factors of well-distributed sequences
Poisson–Dirichlet law under level-one distribution; relevant background, not an unconditional Carmichael counting solution.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11884

9. Wright (2026), (a,a)-Carmichael numbers and greatest common divisors of p-a
Near-linear generalized counting bound still assumes a strong conjecture about the first prime in an arithmetic progression.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02738

10. jdb19937/carmichael (September 2026), deterministic construction manuscript
Claims a deterministic construction of a Carmichael number beyond n in quasipolynomial time; construction is not an exponent-one counting theorem. Correctness not independently adjudicated here.
https://github.com/jdb19937/carmichael/blob/main/carmichael.tex

11. Gupta, Webster and Webster (October 1, 2026), The Prime Wavelet Tree: Compressing the Carmichael Numbers
Compression of a finite Carmichael tabulation, not an asymptotic density proof.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02550

12. OpenAI (September 2026), Weighted dilation graphs, smooth shifted primes and totient fibers
Prior smooth-prime ingredient used by the submitted work; introduction notes a separate progression-distribution input for the classical Carmichael construction. It does not itself state the full Carmichael-counting conclusion.
https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Weighted-Dilation-Graphs-Smooth-Shifted-Primes-and-Totient-Fibers-September-24-2026/build/sections/00-introduction.tex

13. OpenAI (September 2026), The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis
Prior zero-free ingredient used by the submitted development. No Carmichael statement found in this manuscript. The current submitted mathematical account uses the formal 23/24 zero-free route rather than copying its stronger prose statement.
https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/build/paper.tex

14. Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), Erdős Problems via Bayesian Computation, II
Abstract inspected; discusses AGP density and a remaining derandomisation gap. Full paper could not be fetched (HTTP 403), so it was not cleared by full-text review.
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6913501
